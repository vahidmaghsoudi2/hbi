## 2. Evidence-supported definition and presentation

The American Academy of Dermatology describes dry skin as skin that loses too much water. Reported signs can include flaking, rough texture, itching, peeling, cracking, and discomfort such as stinging or burning. These signs do not independently establish the cause or a diagnosis.

Sources:
- American Academy of Dermatology (AAD), “Dry skin: Overview”: https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/dry-skin-overview
- AAD, “Dry skin: Signs and symptoms”: https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/dry-skin-symptoms

**Evidence status:** SUPPORTED for the general description and listed presentations by the cited dermatology guidance.  
**Limit:** This is a general clinical description, not a validated HBI definition or proof that every listed sign should become a separate HBI problem/need.

## 3. Potential observable signals (not a diagnostic checklist)

Record only what the person reports or the operator observes, keeping the source explicit:
- Reported feeling of dryness or tightness.
- Visible flaking or rough surface.
- Itching, stinging, or discomfort.
- Peeling or cracking.
- Location, onset, duration, recurrence, and factors the person says worsen or relieve it.

AAD guidance notes that persistent or worsening dryness can have causes beyond dry air, including underlying conditions or medication effects. Therefore, the HBI intake must not infer cause from the presenting concern alone:
https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/dry-skin-overview

## 5. Evidence-backed general care context — not product selection

AAD public guidance supports general measures such as short warm (not hot) bathing, gentle cleansing, and applying a fragrance-free moisturizer while skin is still damp. Creams or ointments may be more effective than lotions for very dry skin. These are general-care statements; they do **not** establish that any specific product is suitable for a particular person.

Source: AAD, “Dermatologists' top tips for relieving dry skin”:
https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/dry/dermatologists-tips-relieve-dry-skin

**HBI use boundary:** This information may inform a reviewed educational reference after human review. It must not be converted into an automatic product recommendation, a diagnosis, or a claim that a product containing a named ingredient will resolve the problem.

## 6. Safety / referral boundary

This packet does not define a diagnostic or triage algorithm. The reference should clearly state that persistent, worsening, severe, painful, or markedly irritated symptoms—or signs suggesting infection—need assessment by an appropriately qualified health professional rather than an automated product match. AAD guidance advises dermatology assessment when dryness persists despite self-care or worsens:
https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/dry/dermatologists-tips-relieve-dry-skin
